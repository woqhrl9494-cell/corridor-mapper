Methods & scope

EchoMap compares two non-learning methods for collaborative bistatic range-only wall mapping. Each method receives measured vehicle positions, their covariances and unordered path ranges, one snapshot at a time. Wall geometry and reflection labels are available only to the simulator and evaluator.

Simulated scene → measured rangesMeasured prefix → candidate decisionsDecisions + ground truth → evaluation

Guarded PSPT

DRF ridges initialise local quadratic patches and mirror alternatives. Four hypotheses (neither, original, mirror, both) accumulate predictive evidence before geometry updates. Geometry and own-patch evidence guards require at least two informative matched snapshots. Ambiguous geometry can remain held.

Fixed-fragment B

DRF ridges initialise fixed 2 m straight fragments. Gaussian range likelihoods share each echo between competing candidates, then cap the total assignment within a link. A fragment is scored for 20 subsequent snapshots, followed by a 5-snapshot regeneration cooldown. Selection uses the highest score attained so far after at least two matching snapshots. It does not implement a rejection decision.

Comparison protocol

Both methods use a 100 × 100 candidate grid, a 4σ residual band and exact ellipse perimeter. Their models and computational costs differ: PSPT updates geometry and paired hypotheses; B holds geometry fixed. B uses the declared range SD in its Gaussian variance (the previous experiment used 0.1 m). Display-grid settings affect only the shared field plots.

The fixed development log-score thresholds are 6.4615384615384635 for PSPT and 12 for B. Scores have different meanings and scales. These are uncalibrated diagnostic operating points; neither method obtained an LTT-certified threshold in the earlier calibration study. This page does not claim an error-rate guarantee or use the displayed ground truth to adjust thresholds.

Reading the results

Centre precision measures supported centres within 0.4 m of true walls. Observed recall measures supported-patch coverage within 0.4 m of wall samples lying within 1 m of past generated reflection points. Surface F₁ combines sampled-patch precision and observed recall. Empty precision is undefined; recall is zero when observed walls exist but the estimate is empty. These metrics differ from the whole-fragment grouped FP loss used in the previous tuning study.

Solid segments are supported patches; they are never snapped to true walls or joined across unsupported gaps. The dashed wall is evaluation truth. Held candidates express insufficient or ambiguous evidence. A supported candidate can still be wrong.

Fields and simulation

D‾=Dmax(Q,1),βˆ=DA+εA

β̂ is a field-contrast index, not a probability. Walls use a shared cubic B-spline centreline with independent upper and lower perturbations. The estimation domain is [0, 80] × [−20, 50] m. Scene generation enforces a 4 m minimum vertical passage width and 1 m vertical vehicle clearance. Vehicles advance ⅔ m in x per snapshot, covering 80 m over 120 snapshots from their initial positions. Beyond the finite wall ends, the centreline follows its endpoint tangent.

Validation is synthetic and numerical. The tool does not establish performance with physical sensors or in real caves. The source repository retains the previous implementation and validation evidence.
